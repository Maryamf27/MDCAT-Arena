@ 6.1
E|What are enzymes?|Biological catalysts, mostly proteins|Storage polysaccharides|Hormones that carry messages|Structural lipids of membranes|Enzymes speed up metabolic reactions without being used up.
E|What happens to an enzyme at the end of the reaction it catalyses?|It is unchanged and can be reused|It is destroyed|It becomes the product|It turns into the substrate|Enzymes are not consumed, so small amounts can catalyse many reactions.
E|How do enzymes speed up reactions?|By lowering the activation energy|By raising the temperature|By adding energy to substrates|By changing the final products|Enzymes provide an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

M|How does an enzyme affect the equilibrium of a reversible reaction?|It speeds up both directions but does not change the equilibrium position|It shifts the equilibrium to the product side|It shifts the equilibrium to the substrate side|It makes the reaction irreversible|Catalysts reach equilibrium faster without altering it.

H|What is a holoenzyme?|An apoenzyme combined with its cofactor|A protein without a cofactor|An enzyme-inhibitor complex|An enzyme-substrate complex|The inactive protein part (apoenzyme) plus a cofactor forms the active holoenzyme.
H|Which statement about ribozymes is correct?|They are RNA molecules with catalytic activity|They are proteins that make RNA|They are lipids that digest proteins|They are non-living enzymes in rocks|Ribozymes show that some biological catalysts are not proteins.

@ 6.4
