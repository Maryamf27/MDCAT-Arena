# Biology Unit 15 Homeostasis
@ 15.1
E|Which organs make up the human urinary system?|Kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder and urethra|Kidneys, liver, bladder and rectum|Kidneys, ureters, stomach and urethra|Liver, ureters, bladder and anus|Urine is formed in the kidneys and carried out through the ureters, bladder and urethra.
E|What is the structural and functional unit of the kidney?|Nephron|Neuron|Alveolus|Sarcomere|Each kidney contains about one million nephrons.
E|Which region of the kidney lies immediately beneath the capsule?|Renal cortex|Renal pelvis|Renal medulla|Calyx|The cortex contains the renal corpuscles and convoluted tubules.
M|The renal corpuscle consists of|Glomerulus and Bowman's capsule|Loop of Henle and collecting duct|Proximal and distal tubule|Renal pelvis and ureter|Filtration takes place in the corpuscle in the cortex.
M|Which part of the nephron creates the concentration gradient in the medulla?|Loop of Henle|Bowman's capsule|Glomerulus|Proximal convoluted tubule|The loop acts as a countercurrent multiplier.
M|How does urine move from the kidney to the bladder?|Peristalsis of the ureter|Gravity only|Diffusion|Ciliary beating|Smooth muscle waves push urine along.
M|Into which structure do the collecting ducts drain urine?|Renal pelvis through the renal papillae|Bowman's capsule|Glomerulus|Urethra directly|Urine passes to calyces, the pelvis and the ureter.
H|Why are juxtamedullary nephrons important for producing concentrated urine?|They have long loops of Henle that extend deep into the medulla|They have no tubules|They lack glomeruli|They are located in the pelvis|Long loops sustain a steep osmotic gradient.
H|Why is the efferent arteriole narrower than the afferent arteriole?|It maintains high pressure in the glomerulus|It reduces blood flow|It carries lymph|It produces urine|The resistance keeps pressure high for filtration.
H|What is the role of podocytes in the glomerular filtration barrier?|Their filtration slits restrict passage of large proteins|They secrete hormones|They absorb glucose|They contract the tubule|Podocytes form the inner layer of Bowman's capsule.
@ 15.2
E|Where does glomerular filtration take place?|Between the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule|Loop of Henle|Collecting duct|Bladder|Blood pressure forces fluid out of the glomerular capillaries.
E|Where does most selective reabsorption occur?|Proximal convoluted tubule|Collecting duct|Bladder|Ureter|About 65 percent of filtrate is reabsorbed there.
E|What is the normal glomerular filtration rate?|About 125 mL per minute|About 5 mL per minute|About 500 mL per minute|About 1 mL per minute|It is about 180 litres per day.
M|How is glucose reabsorbed in the proximal tubule?|By active transport|By osmosis|By simple diffusion|It is not reabsorbed|All filtered glucose is normally reabsorbed.
M|What is tubular secretion?|Movement of substances from blood into the tubule|Movement from the tubule to blood|Filtration at the glomerulus|Formation of urea|Hydrogen ions, potassium and drugs are secreted.
M|Which substances are normally absent from the glomerular filtrate?|Blood cells and large proteins|Glucose|Urea|Water|Filtration is selective by size.
M|Which part of the loop of Henle is permeable to water?|Descending limb|Ascending limb|Both are impermeable|Neither|Water leaves the descending limb.
H|What happens when blood glucose exceeds the renal threshold of about 180 mg per dL?|Glucose appears in the urine|Glucose is stored|Urine volume decreases|Glucose is destroyed|Carriers are saturated.
H|Why might protein in the urine indicate kidney damage?|The filtration barrier has become leaky|The tubules are secreting protein|Proteins are normal|The bladder makes protein|Large proteins should not be filtered.
H|How does ADH reduce urine volume?|It inserts aquaporins in the collecting duct to increase water reabsorption|It increases filtration|It blocks the loop|It destroys urea|The urine becomes concentrated.
@ 15.3
E|What is excretion?|Removal of metabolic waste products|Removal of undigested food|Intake of nutrients|Secretion of hormones|Kidneys excrete urea and others.
E|What is osmoregulation?|Regulation of water and salt balance|Regulation of temperature|Regulation of blood sugar|Regulation of pH only|Kidneys adjust urine concentration.
E|Which hormone controls water reabsorption in the collecting ducts?|Antidiuretic hormone|Insulin|Glucagon|Thyroxine|ADH is released by the posterior pituitary.
M|What happens to ADH and urine in dehydration?|ADH rises and urine is concentrated|ADH falls and urine dilute|No change|ADH rises and urine dilute|Water is conserved.
M|What happens after drinking a large amount of water?|ADH falls and a large volume of dilute urine is produced|ADH rises|Urine stops|Urine concentrated|Less water is reabsorbed.
M|Why does alcohol increase urine output?|It inhibits ADH release|It stimulates ADH|It increases filtration only|It blocks the ureter|The result is diuresis.
M|What does aldosterone do?|Increases sodium reabsorption in the distal tubule|Decreases sodium reabsorption|Increases potassium retention|Reduces blood volume|It is secreted by the adrenal cortex.
H|Diabetes insipidus is characterised by|Large volumes of dilute urine due to lack of ADH action|Glucose in urine|Small volumes of urine|Blood in urine|The collecting duct stays impermeable to water.
H|Where are osmoreceptors that monitor blood osmolarity located?|Hypothalamus|Kidney cortex|Liver|Heart|They trigger ADH release and thirst.
H|Which kidney function is an example of osmoregulation rather than excretion?|Varying urine concentration with hydration|Removal of urea|Removal of creatinine|Removal of drugs|Adjusting water output keeps blood osmolarity constant.
@ 15.4
E|Which vessel supplies blood to the glomerulus?|Afferent arteriole|Efferent arteriole|Renal vein|Vasa recta|It arises from the renal artery branches.
E|What is the main function of the glomerular capillaries?|Filtration of blood|Reabsorption|Secretion only|Urine storage|They have fenestrated walls.
E|What is the main function of peritubular capillaries?|Reabsorption and secretion|Filtration|Urine production|Hormone synthesis|They surround the tubules.
M|Why is reabsorption favoured in peritubular capillaries?|Low hydrostatic and high oncotic pressure|High hydrostatic pressure|No pressure|Absence of proteins|Blood pressure is about 10 mmHg.
M|What is special about the glomerular capillary bed?|It lies between two arterioles|It lies between two veins|It lies between artery and vein|It has no walls|The afferent and efferent vessels are both arterioles.
M|From which vessel do peritubular capillaries arise?|Efferent arteriole|Afferent arteriole|Renal vein|Renal pelvis|They carry blood away from the glomerulus.
M|What are the vasa recta?|Capillaries running alongside the loop of Henle|Glomerular capillaries|Ureter branches|Collecting ducts|They preserve the medullary gradient.
H|Why is oncotic pressure high in peritubular capillaries?|Plasma proteins remain after filtration|They contain more water|They lack proteins|They hold urea|Concentrated proteins draw water back.
H|What happens if the efferent arteriole constricts?|Glomerular pressure and filtration rate increase|Pressure decreases|Filtration stops|Reabsorption increases|Resistance raises upstream pressure.
H|Why is peritubular capillary pressure low?|Blood has already passed through two arterioles|It has no vessel|It is near the heart|It has no cells|Resistance of afferent and efferent vessels reduces pressure.
@ 15.5

E|Which factor increases the risk of kidney stones?|Low water intake|High water intake|Low salt|Vitamin A|Concentrated urine favours crystals.
M|What type of stone is related to high purine intake and gout?|Uric acid stones|Struvite stones|Cystine stones|Glucose stones|Uric acid is poorly soluble in acidic urine.
M|How are small stones managed?|Hydration and pain relief so they pass naturally|Immediate surgery|Antibiotics only|Ignoring|Most small stones pass in the urine.
M|What is ESWL?|Shock waves used to break stones|Surgical removal|Dialysis|Antibiotic therapy|Extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy.
M|Which stones are associated with urinary infection?|Struvite|Calcium oxalate|Uric acid|Cystine|Urease-producing bacteria raise pH.
H|Which condition raises urinary calcium and stone risk?|Hyperparathyroidism|Hypothyroidism|Anaemia|Hypoglycaemia|It increases calcium release.
H|How are large stones usually removed?|Percutaneous nephrolithotomy or ureteroscopy|Painkillers|Diet|Antacids|Endoscopic procedures fragment or remove stones.
H|How can uric acid stones be prevented?|Alkalinising the urine|Acidifying the urine|Decreasing water|Eating more purines|Uric acid is more soluble at higher pH.

H|Which drugs can be nephrotoxic?|NSAIDs and aminoglycosides|Vitamin C|Water|Paracetamol at normal dose only|They damage tubules.
H|Polycystic kidney disease is|An inherited cause of kidney failure|An infection|A tumour|An autoimmune disease|Cysts replace functioning tissue.
H|Which dialysis uses the patient's peritoneum as the membrane?|Peritoneal dialysis|Haemodialysis|Transplant|Lithotripsy|Dialysate is placed in the abdominal cavity.
