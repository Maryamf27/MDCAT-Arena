# Biology Unit 4 Cell Structure and Function
@ 4.1
E|Which structure is found in plant cells but not in animal cells?|Cell wall made of cellulose|Plasma membrane|Ribosomes|Mitochondria|Plant cells are surrounded by a rigid cellulose wall outside the plasma membrane; animal cells have no wall.
E|Which organelle carries out photosynthesis in a plant cell?|Chloroplast|Mitochondrion|Golgi apparatus|Lysosome|Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and are found in green plant cells but not in animal cells.
E|A mature plant cell typically has which kind of vacuole?|One large central vacuole|Many tiny contractile vacuoles only|No vacuoles at all|Vacuoles that contain DNA|Mature plant cells have a large central vacuole, while animal cells have small, temporary ones.
M|Which organelle is present in animal cells but absent from most higher plant cells?|Centriole|Nucleus|Endoplasmic reticulum|Ribosome|Centrioles organise the spindle in animal cells; higher plants form spindles without centrioles.
M|What is the main storage carbohydrate in plant cells and in animal cells respectively?|Starch and glycogen|Glycogen and starch|Cellulose and chitin|Sucrose and lactose|Plants store starch, animals store glycogen.
M|How does cytokinesis differ between plant and animal cells?|Plant cells form a cell plate; animal cells form a cleavage furrow|Animal cells form a cell plate; plant cells form a cleavage furrow|Neither cell type divides its cytoplasm|Both divide by budding|The rigid wall of plant cells requires construction of a new wall, the cell plate, between daughter cells.
M|Why do plant cells have a fixed, regular shape while animal cells are often irregular?|The cellulose cell wall gives rigid support|Plant cells have no cytoskeleton|Animal cells lack plasma membranes|Plant cells contain no water|The wall prevents deformation and gives characteristic polygonal shapes.
H|Which structure forms cytoplasmic channels linking adjacent plant cells across their walls?|Plasmodesmata|Desmosomes|Gap junctions|Tight junctions|Plasmodesmata are plant-specific cytoplasmic bridges; animal cells use gap junctions.
H|A red blood cell bursts in distilled water, but a plant cell in distilled water does not. Why?|The plant cell wall resists swelling and prevents bursting|Plant cells have no water-permeable membrane|Plant cells actively pump out all water|Red blood cells lack cytoplasm|The wall exerts turgor pressure that balances the osmotic inflow of water.
H|What is the name of the membrane that surrounds the central vacuole of a plant cell?|Tonoplast|Plasmalemma|Nuclear envelope|Thylakoid membrane|The tonoplast is the vacuolar membrane; the plasmalemma is the plasma membrane.
@ 4.2
E|Which feature is characteristic of prokaryotic cells?|No membrane-bound nucleus|Presence of a nucleolus|Mitochondria|Endoplasmic reticulum|Prokaryotic DNA lies free in the cytoplasm in a nucleoid region.
E|What is the size of ribosomes in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cytoplasm respectively?|70S and 80S|80S and 70S|60S and 40S|30S and 50S|Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes; eukaryotic cytosolic ribosomes are 80S.
E|Which of these organisms is a prokaryote?|Escherichia coli|Yeast|Amoeba|Paramecium|E. coli is a bacterium; the others are eukaryotes.
M|What is the main component of the bacterial cell wall?|Peptidoglycan|Cellulose|Chitin|Lignin|Bacterial walls contain peptidoglycan (murein); plants use cellulose and fungi use chitin.
M|How is the DNA of a typical bacterium organised?|A single circular chromosome in the nucleoid, often with plasmids|Several linear chromosomes in a nucleus|DNA wrapped around histones in nucleosomes|Only RNA without DNA|Bacterial chromosomes are usually circular and not enclosed by a nuclear membrane.
M|Which statement about cell size is correct?|Eukaryotic cells are generally larger than prokaryotic cells|Prokaryotic cells are always larger than eukaryotic cells|Both are always the same size|Prokaryotes are about 100 micrometres on average|Typical bacteria measure 1-10 micrometres, whereas eukaryotic cells are about 10-100 micrometres.
M|Which organelle is absent from prokaryotic cells?|Mitochondria|Ribosomes|Plasma membrane|Cytoplasm|Prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles, so respiration occurs at the plasma membrane.
H|Which observation supports the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria?|They have their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes|They have a nuclear envelope|They contain histones in nucleosomes|They are 80S in size|Resemblance to bacteria in DNA and ribosomes suggests origin from engulfed prokaryotes.
H|Which pair correctly gives the usual mode of cell division in prokaryotes and eukaryotes?|Binary fission and mitosis|Mitosis and binary fission|Budding and meiosis|Meiosis and fragmentation|Bacteria divide by binary fission; eukaryotic body cells divide by mitosis.
H|How does a bacterial flagellum differ from a eukaryotic flagellum?|It is made of the protein flagellin without a 9+2 microtubule arrangement|It has a 9+2 arrangement of microtubules|It is enclosed by the plasma membrane|It beats in a wave using dynein|The bacterial flagellum is a rigid rotating filament of flagellin.
@ 4.3
E|Which organelle contains the chromatin and directs the activities of the cell?|Nucleus|Lysosome|Vacuole|Ribosome|The nucleus holds the genetic material and controls cell activities.
E|Which organelle is known as the powerhouse of the cell?|Mitochondrion|Golgi apparatus|Nucleolus|Peroxisome|Mitochondria carry out aerobic respiration and produce most of the cell's ATP.
E|What distinguishes the rough endoplasmic reticulum from the smooth endoplasmic reticulum?|Ribosomes are attached to its surface|It has no membrane|It lacks cisternae|It is found only in plants|Ribosomes on RER synthesise proteins for secretion or membranes.
M|What is the main function of the Golgi apparatus?|Modify, sort and package proteins and lipids for secretion|Synthesise ATP by oxidative phosphorylation|Replicate DNA|Make rRNA|The Golgi receives vesicles from the ER, modifies their contents and dispatches them.
M|Which function is typical of the smooth ER?|Lipid synthesis and detoxification|Protein synthesis on attached ribosomes|Photosynthesis|Spindle formation|SER makes lipids and steroids and detoxifies drugs in liver cells.
M|Where are the enzymes of the electron transport chain and ATP synthase located in mitochondria?|Inner mitochondrial membrane|Outer mitochondrial membrane|Mitochondrial matrix only|Intermembrane space only|The cristae of the inner membrane carry the respiratory chain and ATP synthase.
M|What are nuclear pores and nucleolus respectively concerned with?|Passage of molecules between nucleus and cytoplasm; ribosomal RNA synthesis|Photosynthesis; protein digestion|Lipid storage; ATP synthesis|Cell division; excretion|Pores regulate nucleocytoplasmic transport; the nucleolus makes rRNA and assembles ribosome subunits.
H|What is the correct route of a secretory protein?|Rough ER, transport vesicle, Golgi, secretory vesicle, plasma membrane|Golgi, rough ER, nucleus, plasma membrane|Lysosome, Golgi, nucleus, vacuole|Nucleus, mitochondrion, ribosome, plasma membrane|Proteins made on the RER are transported by vesicles to the Golgi for processing and then exported.
H|Why do cardiac muscle cells have many mitochondria with extensive cristae?|The folds increase membrane area for ATP production to meet high energy demand|Cristae store calcium for contraction|Cristae digest worn-out proteins|Cristae synthesise myosin|More inner membrane means more electron transport chains and ATP synthase.
H|The sarcoplasmic reticulum of muscle is a specialised form of which organelle, and what does it store?|Smooth ER; calcium ions|Rough ER; sodium ions|Golgi; glycogen|Lysosome; myosin|The sarcoplasmic reticulum releases Ca2+ to trigger contraction.
@ 4.4
E|Which two chemical components make up chromatin?|DNA and histone proteins|RNA and lipids|Starch and proteins|Cellulose and DNA|Chromatin is DNA complexed with histones and other proteins.
E|How many chromosomes are present in a normal human somatic cell?|46|23|44|48|Humans have 23 pairs, giving 46 chromosomes in body cells.
E|To which part of the chromosome do spindle fibres attach?|Centromere|Telomere|Nucleolus organiser|Chromatid arm|Spindle microtubules attach through the kinetochore at the centromere.
M|What is a nucleosome?|DNA wrapped around a core of eight histone proteins|A bundle of microtubules|A ribosome subunit|A membrane vesicle|About 146 base pairs of DNA coil around a histone octamer, the first level of DNA packaging.
M|Chromosomes are classified as metacentric, submetacentric, acrocentric or telocentric according to|Position of the centromere|Number of genes|Length of telomeres|Number of histones|The centromere position determines the relative arm lengths.
M|What distinguishes heterochromatin from euchromatin?|Heterochromatin is tightly condensed and transcriptionally inactive|Heterochromatin is loosely packed and active|Heterochromatin lacks DNA|Heterochromatin is found only in the nucleolus|Euchromatin is loose and active; heterochromatin is dense and mostly inactive.
M|What is the function of telomeres?|Protect chromosome ends from degradation and fusion|Attach to spindle fibres|Code for ribosomal RNA|Join sister chromatids|Telomeres cap the ends of linear chromosomes and maintain their integrity.
H|How many chromatids are present in a human cell in G2 phase of the cell cycle?|92|46|23|184|After S phase each of the 46 chromosomes consists of two sister chromatids.
H|Which amino acids make histones positively charged and able to bind DNA?|Lysine and arginine|Glutamate and aspartate|Glycine and alanine|Cysteine and methionine|Basic amino acids attract the negatively charged phosphate backbone of DNA.
H|What is the difference between sister chromatids and homologous chromosomes?|Sister chromatids are identical copies joined at a centromere; homologues are a maternal and paternal pair|Sister chromatids come from different parents|Homologues are identical copies from replication|Sister chromatids differ in genes|Replication produces genetically identical sister chromatids; homologues are separate chromosomes of a pair that carry the same genes.
